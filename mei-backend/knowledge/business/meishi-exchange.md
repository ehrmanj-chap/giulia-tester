# Business Card Etiquette (Meishi Exchange)

Source seed: Kelsie Kato's Mei Business Intelligence research folder, file “Business Card Etiquette (Meishi Exchange) - Additional Research.pdf”, updated September 7, 2026.

The research describes physical business cards (meishi) as still important in many Japanese professional settings even as digital substitutes expand, especially in technology and remote work.

Practical handling guidance documented in the research includes:
- Avoid writing on a received card in the giver's presence.
- Avoid immediately putting a received card into a trouser pocket or treating it casually.
- A dedicated cardholder is a safer default than a wallet or loose pocket.
- Avoid folding or rough handling.

The research cites a standard Japanese business-card size of about 91 mm × 55 mm, slightly different from the common US size. Treat this as a practical printing detail rather than an etiquette requirement.

The source also notes a broader group-oriented design tendency: Japanese meishi often foreground company/department information rather than treating the card mainly as a vehicle for individual personal branding. This is useful context for why the physical object can carry representational weight, but it should not be turned into a claim about every company or every card design.

Digital QR codes and contact-sharing tools increasingly supplement or sometimes replace paper cards, particularly in tech and video-call contexts. The research explicitly characterizes this as gradual displacement rather than complete replacement.

When advising users, distinguish between a conservative “safe default” for a formal first meeting and what may be normal in a younger, international, or digital-first company.
