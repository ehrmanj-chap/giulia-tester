# Shinto, Buddhism and Spirituality

Source seed: Kelsie Kato's Mei Cultural Intelligence research folder, file “Shinto, Buddhism and Spirituality.pdf”, updated August 28, 2026.

Japan's religious landscape is often characterized by layered participation rather than mutually exclusive affiliation. The source cites Agency for Cultural Affairs survey figures in which reported Shinto and Buddhist adherent counts together exceed Japan's total population, partly because shrine/temple affiliation measures are loose and overlapping rather than equivalent to an exclusive personal declaration of belief.

A practical implication is that Shinto and Buddhist customs may coexist within the same household and life cycle. The source describes common patterns such as shrine observance around birth or New Year and Buddhist funeral rites, while also noting that many people describe themselves as not strongly religious. Avoid treating this as a rigid template for every household.

Shinto is Japan's indigenous tradition centered on kami associated with nature, ancestors, and places, generally observed at shrines. Buddhism arrived from the Asian mainland in the sixth century and is generally practiced at temples; the traditions were historically intertwined for centuries despite later institutional separation.

Visitor etiquette summarized by the source:
- At a shrine or temple, follow posted rules and behave quietly and respectfully around worshippers.
- At a shrine purification fountain, rinse hands and mouth without touching the ladle to the lips when that facility is in use and instructions support it.
- At a Shinto shrine, a common prayer sequence is two bows, two claps, silent prayer, and one final bow.
- At a Buddhist temple, prayer is generally quieter and does not use the Shinto clapping sequence.
- Photography restrictions vary by site and should be followed.

Treat these as common conventions rather than universal rules. Individual shrines and temples can differ, and posted local instructions take precedence over generalized etiquette.
